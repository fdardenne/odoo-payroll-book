Chapter 18Part IV · How the payroll engine works

The engine in one picture

What goes into a payslip, what comes out, and the short loop in between

After this chapter you can
  • Say in one sentence what the payroll engine does
  • Name what goes into a payslip and where its lines go afterwards
  • Read the engine's core loop and explain why it is the same in every country
  • Name the five records you work with, who owns each, and how often each changes
  • Tell which records ship with a country module and which belong to the customer

Mélanie's February payslip has 44 lines; Marie's, on the same structure, has 49 (her car, her meal vouchers). A Mexican weekly payslip looks nothing like either. Yet nobody wrote a "Belgian payslip program" or a "Mexican" one. One small engine read a list of rules and applied them, one after the other. This part of the book explains that engine at the level you need to create payroll records and to review them.

What the payroll engine is

The payroll engine is the part of Odoo that turns one employee's data for one period into payslip lines. It takes the rules of one structure and applies them, in order, to the employee's contract terms, time and inputs.

Three things follow from that definition:

  • The engine knows no country. Everything a law says is in the rules and their parameters, which are data. The engine only runs them.
  • The engine decides nothing. Whether a line appears, and how much it is, is decided by a rule. When a payslip is wrong, the cause is a rule or the data it read, almost never the engine.
  • It runs again on demand. A draft payslip is recomputed whenever you click Compute or its data changes. A validated payslip is frozen (Chapter 15).

In practice

When a payslip looks wrong, follow the figure from the left: is the version right (wage, situation, structure type)? Are the worked days right? Are the inputs right? Only then open a rule. Most "rule bugs" are data in one of the first three.

Why a payroll engine at all?

Payroll law differs in every country and changes every year. If each country's payslip were a program, every new tax table would be a code change, and every customer who pays a sector bonus would need a developer. Odoo takes the other road: rules are data.

A salary rule is a record: a name, a code, a condition and a small formula, usually a few lines of Python. Country modules ship their rules in data files; a customer can create more from the screen. One engine evaluates all of them the same way, giving each rule a dictionary of what it may see: the version, the worked days, the totals so far (Chapter 19 lists them). Here is that engine, simplified to its core:

def compute_lines(payslip):
    seen = payslip._get_localdict()             # what a rule may read
    lines = []
    for rule in sorted(payslip.struct_id.rule_ids, key=lambda r: r.sequence):
        if not rule._satisfy_condition(seen):   # condition false: no line at all
            continue
        amount, quantity, rate = rule._compute_rule(seen)[:3]
        total = amount * quantity * rate / 100
        seen['result_rules'][rule.code] = {'total': total, 'amount': amount}
        rule.category_ids._sum_salary_rule_category(seen, total)   # running totals
        lines.append({'code': rule.code, 'amount': amount, 'quantity': quantity,
                      'rate': rate, 'total': total})
    return lines

For each rule, in order: test the condition; if it holds, compute an amount, a quantity and a rate; multiply them into a total; remember the result for later rules; add it to the rule's running totals (its categories, Chapter 20); keep one line. The real method, _get_payslip_lines, adds batches of payslips, year-to-date sums and a few hooks for countries, but the whole engine fits on one page; the names may change, the loop does not. That is why you can review any country's payroll once you can read its rules.

Key idea

The engine is small and stable; the rules are many and change every year. Everything in this part is about reading and placing rules, because that is where every country's law lives.

Two more long-lived concepts surround them: the employee's version, which the customer fills in (Chapter 13), and the time types, which the country module ships and which say how each kind of hour is paid (Chapter 14).

Notice the Changes lines: the records change at very different speeds. A parameter changes every year; a rule only when the method changes; a structure type almost never. Keeping each fact in the record that changes at its speed is what turns a new tax year into a data update instead of a rewrite.

Common pitfall: fixing one employee in a shared rule

A rule is shared by every employee on its structures. Editing the ONSS rule because one employee's line looks wrong changes everyone's payslips, and stops the rule from receiving Odoo's fixes (Chapter 21). Look for the cause in that employee's version, worked days or inputs first.

Five design ideas

Everything in the next three chapters follows from five decisions.

1 · Rules are data
A rule is a record with a small formula, not a program. Countries ship rules as data; customers add their own from the screen; one engine runs them all.
Wheresalary rules and structuresChapter 19
2 · Legal numbers live in dated parameters
A rule asks for "the cap" by its code and gets the value valid on the payslip's date. Next year's law is a new value, never an edit.
Whererule parametersChapters 11, 21
3 · Time is computed, not stored
Work entries are derived from the schedule and time off whenever a payslip needs them, so a late change of leave is never forgotten.
Wherework entries and worked daysChapter 14
4 · Totals connect the rules
Rules do not call each other. Each one adds to running totals (gross, taxable base, net), and later rules read the totals.
Wheresalary categoriesChapter 20
5 · Countries are layers on a shared framework
The engine, payslips, pay runs, accounting and declarations are shared. A country module adds rules, parameters, categories, fields and its filings on top, and never replaces the engine.

Around the world

The same five records carry very different laws:

  • Mexico: one structure type, two structures (regular pay and the Christmas bonus), frequencies from weekly to monthly.
  • Belgium: about ten structures for one structure type, because holiday pay, the 13th month and termination are separate payslips.
  • India: a second structure type for interns.
  • United States: one structure holding the income-tax rule of every state; each rule's condition tests the employee's work address.
  • Netherlands: a small module that is almost only data.

Review checklist

Before reviewing a payroll database or a country module, sort its records by owner:

  • Ships with the country module and is updated by Odoo: structure types, structures, rules, categories, parameters and their values, the payroll settings of time types, dashboard warnings.
  • Belongs to the customer: employees and their versions, time off, inputs, company settings, and any rule or structure they created (Odoo marks those as created by a user).
  • A shipped record the customer edited no longer receives Odoo's updates (Chapter 21). List them; each one needs a reason.
  • A legal number typed in a customer record, or a customer-specific amount in a shipped rule, is in the wrong place.

Summary

  • The payroll engine turns one employee's version, time and inputs for one period into payslip lines, by applying the rules of one structure in order.
  • Accounting, declarations and reports read the lines; they never recompute.
  • Rules are data: small formulas stored as records, shipped by country modules or created by users, run by one engine that fits on a page.
  • Each line is amount × quantity × rate ÷ 100, and its total is added to the rule's categories for later rules.
  • Five records decide a payslip: structure type, structure, salary rule, salary category, rule parameter. They change at different speeds, from almost never to every year.
  • The country module owns the configuration; the customer owns versions, time and inputs; a shipped record the customer edited stops receiving updates.
