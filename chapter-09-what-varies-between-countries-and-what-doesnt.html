Chapter 9Part II · Localization theory

What varies between countries, and what doesn't

Four layers, ten axes of variation, and a comparison grid of Odoo's localizations read from the code

After this chapter you can
  • Place any payroll fact in one of four layers: framework, country, company, employee
  • Name what every country shares and the axes along which countries differ
  • Read the comparison grid and say what each module teaches
  • Recognise the four places a rate can live: XML parameters, downloaded parameters, customer-maintained records, contract fields

Open three payslips in Odoo: Monterrey, Sydney, Namur. Each has earnings, contributions, a tax line and a net, computed by the same engine. Yet the Mexican one caps its social-security wage in a legal reference unit, the Australian one has no employee contribution, and the Belgian one annualises the month. Before you write or review a localization, know which part Odoo gives you and which part is yours to build.

The four layers

Every payroll fact lives in one of four layers. The question to ask is: who decides this value, and how often does it change?

Employee
HR, per person · changes at hire, raise, family event, exit
e.g. wage, family situation, company carhr.version fields
Company
The employer or its accountant · changes rarely
e.g. pay frequency, sector agreement, bank and journalssettings, company fields
Country
The legislator, then the localization team · changes yearly, sometimes mid-year
e.g. contribution rates, tax scale, declarationsl10n_xx_hr_payroll
Framework
Odoo R&D · changes with each release
e.g. payslips, the rule engine, dated parameters, pay runshr_payroll

Each layer is built on the one below it: a company configures what its country's module allows, and an employee's values fill fields the country declared. When a value has to go somewhere, put it in the highest layer where it is still true: a legal rate in the country, an employer's choice in the company, a person's situation on the employee.

What is the same everywhere

However exotic a country's payroll looks, it is built from the same five ideas. Odoo implements each idea once, in the framework, and every localization reuses it. Learn these five and you can open any country module and know where to look.

1 · Time becomes money
A month of salary pays a month of work. Days not worked, paid leave and overtime change the amount, each at its own rate.
Framework piecework entries and worked daysChapters 5, 14
2 · Totals feed the formulas
A contribution or a tax is a percentage of a total of payslip lines: the gross, the contribution base, the taxable base.
Framework piecesalary rule categoriesChapters 2, 20
3 · Rates change over time
Every year brings new rates and tax scales, but an old payslip must keep the rates of its own period.
Framework piecedated rule parametersChapters 11, 21
4 · Every period ends in an entry and a payment
The pay run is booked in the accounts and the net is paid to the bank, every period, the same way.
Framework piecepay runs and payroll accountingChapters 8, 16
5 · The authorities want reports
What was paid and withheld is reported to the tax office and social security, monthly, quarterly or yearly.
Framework piecethe declaration modelChapters 7, 17

The further right a country sits, the more code it needs: a heavy answer on the outputs (declarations, payment files, accounting) and on the data feeds costs far more than a heavy answer on earnings or contributions, which are only more rules.

What the six teach:

  • Size follows declarations and data feeds, not rules. Australia has fewer rules than Mexico but far more code: it reports to the tax office in real time.
  • A rate has four possible homes: a data file (NL, MX), a downloaded official file (BE), records the customer maintains (CH insurers), or a field on the employee's version (SA).
  • The employee side can be empty: Australia has no employee contribution; the UAE has one for nationals only.
  • The accounting module can carry the law: Mexico puts its e-payslip there, Australia its pay-run reporting.

Others (US, CH, SA, HK, KE…) are in research/world_grid.md.

In practice

Before estimating a new country, count its statutory outputs and its sources of rates. Each declaration is a model, an export, a test and a legal source to verify. Rules are cheap; declarations and data feeds are where the weeks go.

Common pitfall

Trusting a grid cell because the rule exists. Reading these modules found an income-tax rule that over-taxes incomes above the second band, tested only on lower salaries. A cell is verified when a golden payslip covers it (Chapter 10), not when the code is there. Chapter 22 lists these patterns.

In Belgium

Belgium sits at the top of most columns: more than 200 sectors with their own agreements, quarterly declarations, a dozen filings. Two of its answers travel: switching rules on and off by sector, and downloading official rates into dated parameters.

Summary

  • Four layers: framework, country, company, employee. Put each fact in the highest layer where it is still true; every payslip needs all four.
  • The framework (hr_payroll) holds the engine, categories, dated parameters, pay runs and hooks; a localization ships rules, data, fields and declarations.
  • Ten axes describe a country, from pay frequency to data feeds.
  • Rates live in four places: XML parameters, downloaded parameters, customer-maintained records, version fields.
  • Size follows declarations and data feeds, not the number of rules.
  • A grid cell is verified by a golden payslip, not by the presence of a rule.
